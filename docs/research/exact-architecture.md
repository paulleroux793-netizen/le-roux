# Architectural Analysis of Two “Exact” Dental Practice Management Systems and Implications for a Custom South African Data Model

Before diving into the details of each product, it is helpful to frame both systems as comprehensive practice management platforms that centralize patient data, clinical documentation, and revenue-cycle workflows in a single database and application layer, even though they are implemented and marketed in different regions and for slightly different primary use cases.[5][11][14][29][5] ExACT Dental Software, distributed via exact-net.com, is positioned in South Africa as an integrated electronic patient record and practice‑management solution with strong emphasis on dental billing, medical aid tariffs, and electronic claims to South African medical schemes.[5][9][9][9][5] EXACT by Software of Excellence (Henry Schein One) is an international platform that emphasizes a tightly integrated appointment book, graphical clinical charting, treatment planning, patient engagement tools such as recalls and portal, and a configurable billing layer that supports private and third‑party payors.[11][14][35][38][29] Despite differences in regional focus and branding, both systems share a common architectural pattern: a patient‑centric database with modules for clinical recording, fee schedules and payors, treatment planning and estimates, invoicing and statements, and claims or scheme reporting, all connected by well‑defined entity relationships and workflow steps that transform clinical events into financial transactions.[9][9][38][9][36] Understanding how these subsystems flow together in each product provides a strong blueprint for designing a custom, single‑practice data model for a South African dental clinic that must handle main medical‑aid members and dependants, treatment planning, chart‑to‑billing linkage, and EDI claims submission and reconciliation.[21][21][49]

## 1. Conceptual Background: Dental Practice Management as an Integrated Platform

A useful starting point is the general definition of dental practice management software (PMS) as articulated by modern vendors. Oryx, for example, defines PMS as the core platform that runs the business and clinical operations of a dental practice, centralizing patient information and supporting front office, clinical, and revenue‑cycle workflows in one place so teams can schedule, chart, diagnose, present treatment, submit claims, and analyze performance without redundant data entry. It emphasizes that the PMS acts as an integration hub over which imaging systems, clearinghouses, payment processors, and other services are layered, allowing data to move reliably across modules while maintaining a single source of truth. Curve Dental similarly describes modern PMS as a connected foundation for appointment scheduling, charting, billing, and patient communications, with cloud‑based access and analytics to improve efficiency and care quality. These general descriptions align closely with how both ExACT Dental Software in South Africa and EXACT by Software of Excellence present themselves, even though they target different geographic markets and regulatory constraints.[5][11][13]

From a systems‑architecture standpoint, this means both products can be viewed as modular applications wrapped around a normalized relational database that stores entities such as patients, providers, appointments, services, treatment plans, invoices, payments, and claims, with each module offering workflow‑specific interfaces onto subsets of that data.[14][33] The clinical charting module reads and writes tooth‑level treatment entities linked to patients and providers; the billing module reads those same treatment entities once they are marked as completed, applies fee schedules based on configured payors or medical schemes, and writes invoice and ledger records; the reporting engine reads from all of these tables to generate financial, clinical, and marketing reports.[2][30][2][2][29] The modules therefore are not separate databases but functional layers over the same record system, which is essential for keeping patient accounts internally consistent and for enabling features like “click through from the chart to the invoice” or “filter care‑manager lists by outstanding treatment with no appointments”.[15][32][9][32]

This integrated design is particularly important in environments with complex third‑party payor structures such as South African medical schemes, where correct coding, tariff selection, and claims submission determine practice cash flow.[21][21][42] Modern claims handling is increasingly automated via electronic data interchange (EDI) between practice systems and switching houses or clearinghouses, using standard formats such as ANSI X12 837 or functionally similar local equivalents.[25][25] Articles describing South African tools like Eminance and GoodX show the pattern: claims are prepared in the PMS, validated against scheme rules, submitted electronically to switching houses, and responses such as claim acknowledgements and remittance advice are received and reconciled in near real time, with the PMS providing the workflow and data structures for all these steps.[21][21][42] ExACT Dental Software explicitly advertises “claims to medical insurance (electronic claims submissions)” and “treatment plan proposals to medical schemes for authorisations”, placing it squarely in this ecosystem.[9][9] EXACT by Software of Excellence, operating largely in UK, ANZ and other markets, instead emphasizes integration with NHS schemes, private plan payors, and reporting categories labeled “Schemes”, but the same architectural approach of payor‑specific fee schedules and scheme reporting applies.[2][2][39][39][2]

In addition, both systems reflect the dental‑specific need for tooth‑ and surface‑level charting, which differentiates dental PMS from more generic medical practice software. EXACT’s odontogram supports permanent, mixed, and deciduous dentitions, base charting, historical treatment and current plan overlays, and quick chart menus for individual teeth.[38][38] ExACT Dental’s clinical charting similarly records oral hygiene (plaque scores), periodontal status, and detailed dental status that becomes the practice’s long‑term electronic patient record.[5] The crucial point for a data‑model perspective is that the tooth chart is not an isolated graphic; behind the scenes each charted procedure becomes a structured record with codes, tooth numbers and surfaces that can be priced, invoiced, and claimed, connecting the clinical view to the billing and claims subsystems.[15][38][36][5]

Therefore, when analysing both ExACT products, it is useful to think in terms of a continuum of data: a patient’s identity and family membership; their scheme membership and payor relationships; their clinical chart and treatment plans; the transformation of planned items into completed treatment; the generation of financial transactions; and finally claims and statements linked to those transactions. Each module in both systems can be mapped onto one or more stages along this continuum.

## 2. ExACT Dental Software (South Africa): Architecture and End‑to‑End Financial–Clinical Flow

### 2.1 High‑level Architecture and Core Modules in ExACT SA

ExACT Dental Software, as marketed on exact-net.com, is described as South Africa’s most popular electronic patient record and practice management software for dentists, echoing its dual role in both clinical record‑keeping and business administration.[5][9] The vendor emphasizes that ExACT is a “modular, yet integrated” system, allowing practices to adopt different functional areas at their own pace while still benefiting from a cohesive flow of information through the practice.[9][9] Marketing descriptions stress that for complete practice management, ExACT integrates front‑office tools such as scheduling and accounting with clinical tools like charting, effectively linking accounting, scheduling and charting functions into one solution for single‑ and multi‑user practices.[5] This implies a client–server architecture typical of on‑premise practice software: a central database instance, multiple workstation clients, and additional components for electronic claim switching and document generation.

The modules highlighted for ExACT SA concentrate strongly on the financial lifecycle of treatment. The “7 Reasons to use ExACT Dental” content notes that ExACT directly influences almost 100% of the flow of finances in a practice, enumerating patient billing, patient invoices and statements, and private and medical insurance claims as core functional areas.[9][9] It further describes treatment estimates or quotations to patients, treatment plan proposals to medical schemes for authorizations, and claims to medical insurance via electronic submissions, together with patient invoices, payment receipts, allocation of payments, and statements.[9][9] These capabilities suggest that ExACT maintains a robust patient ledger concept, where all invoice, receipt, adjustment and claim transactions are tracked per patient and can be summarised into statements on demand or in batch. The presence of a “Fees importer” that ships pre‑configured tariffs for multiple schemes, such as BankMed and Discovery, implies a dedicated tariff module in which medical aid‑specific fee schedules, codes, and reimbursement rules are stored and periodically updated.[49]

On the clinical side, ExACT’s clinical charting function is promoted as the feature that established it as South Africa’s foremost patient record system, including the ability to record plaque scores, periodontal status, detailed dental status, and completed treatment history.[5] This clinical module can be inferred to mirror international odontogram patterns, with tooth‑specific charting that feeds both the clinical audit trail and the financial subsystems. In marketing material, ExACT is also advertised as supporting electronic letters and communications, hinting at a communications module that draws from patient and treatment data to generate templated correspondence, including treatment estimates, scheme pre‑authorisations, and possibly recall letters.[9][9] While public documentation is less detailed than the Software of Excellence help centre, by triangulating its feature list with patterns seen in other SA practice software such as Eminance and GoodX, one can infer that ExACT’s internal architecture includes distinct but tightly coupled subsystems for patient and payor master data, clinical charting, fee schedules, treatment planning, invoicing and ledger management, electronic claims, and reporting.[21][21][42][9]

### 2.2 Patient Record, Medical Aid Membership and Account Structure in ExACT SA

Although ExACT SA’s public documentation does not exhaustively describe its patient data model, several features make its conceptual structure clear. It is positioned as an electronic patient record (EPR) that stores comprehensive clinical and demographic information, which naturally includes identifiers required by South African medical schemes such as scheme name, membership number, main member and dependant codes, and possibly relationship to main member.[5][9][5] The presence of electronic medical‑aid claims and treatment plan proposals to medical schemes implies that the patient master record must be able to identify the correct scheme, option or plan type, and main member details used in EDI claim headers.[9][9] South African medical scheme workflows typically require that every dependant is linked to a single main member on a specific option, with unique beneficiary codes assigned by the scheme, and that claims submitted for a dependant include the main member’s demographic and scheme information.[21][21] In this context, ExACT’s patient record must support an entity relationship where one medical aid membership can have one main member and multiple dependants, each of whom is also an individual patient in the practice database.

A plausible structure is that each patient entity has demographic attributes (name, ID number, contact details), one or more medical aid memberships, and a reference to a “responsible party” for billing, which may be the patient, the main member, or another guarantor. The medical aid membership entity would store scheme code (for example, BankMed), plan or option code, member number, dependant number, and effective dates, similar to how South African medical billing systems like Eminance or GoodX associate patients to schemes and options for automated claims handling.[21][21][42] Because ExACT advertises a fees importer with pre‑configured scheme tariff tables keyed by scheme codes such as BANKMED‑TRA or DISCOVERY, one can infer that the scheme code on the medical aid membership links to a particular fee schedule for pricing purposes.[49] ExACT’s billing module then uses this membership information to determine whether a given treatment line on an invoice should be priced and claimed according to a scheme tariff or according to a private fee schedule for self‑pay patients.

South African guideline tariffs issued by the Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA) and tariff manuals for medical practitioners and dentists establish coding standards and recommend fees for dental services, which practices may use as reference or mapping for their own fee schedules.[31] In addition, the Council for Medical Schemes’ standardisation work describes the adaptation of CMS (Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services) codes and local coding standards for the South African environment. ExACT SA therefore likely stores both practice‑specific service codes and mappings to scheme or HPCSA codes, allowing it to generate claims that comply with scheme tariff structures while preserving the practice’s internal service catalog. The patient record must include identifiers and attributes necessary for these codes to be interpreted correctly by schemes, such as patient age, gender, and in some cases ICD‑10 diagnosis codes, especially for schemes that apply benefit limits or exclusions based on clinical indications.[21][21]

Crucially, the patient account in ExACT SA is not limited to demographic and scheme data; it also functions as the core ledger on which all invoices, payments, and adjustments are recorded. Marketing descriptions that ExACT “directly influences almost 100% of the flow of finances” and “takes care of a practice’s patient billing, invoices and statements, and claims” imply that the patient account holds financial balances and is the foundation for generating statements and tracking outstanding amounts.[9][9] For children or dependants, the account might be configured so that the main member (or other guardian) is the financial responsible party, analogous to the “head of family” concept used in EXACT by Software of Excellence where one family member is designated as the payer for family bills.[22][22][22] This pattern is essential in a South African context, where many dependants are on a single main member’s scheme but may receive treatment at different times and in different combinations, necessitating consolidated billing and statements that follow the payor rather than the individual dependant.

### 2.3 Treatment Planning and Estimates/Quotations in ExACT SA

ExACT SA’s marketing highlights two related capabilities: “treatment estimates (quotations) to patients” and “treatment plan proposals to medical schemes for authorisations”.[9][9] This indicates that ExACT distinguishes between three conceptual layers: the clinical treatment plan created by the dentist, a patient‑facing financial estimate that explains expected costs and cost splits, and a scheme‑facing pre‑authorisation or pre‑determination that uses the same planned codes and fees but is targeted at the funder. The ability to generate these different documents suggests an internal data model where each patient can have multiple treatment plans, each composed of individual treatment items, and each plan can be rendered into one or more estimate or proposal documents associated with that plan.

In a typical path, a dentist first records a clinical examination and charts required treatment in ExACT’s clinical charting screen, linking each planned procedure to appropriate service codes, tooth numbers and surfaces.[5] Each service code is associated with one or more fee schedule entries depending on payor, so when a treatment plan is prepared, ExACT can calculate for each code the scheme tariff, the scheme benefit portion (if available from the tariff table), and the expected patient co‑payment.[49][9][9] For self‑pay patients, the system would apply private fee schedules instead. The “treatment estimates to patients” function then uses this data to produce a quote that may be printed or emailed, showing each planned procedure, the fee, and the breakdown between scheme and patient, similar to how other dental estimate tools aim to show patients their total and out‑of‑pocket costs before treatment. An equivalent capability is described in XDENT’s treatment estimate application, which gives patients an exact idea of treatment cost and how to split costs between them and their medical aid, indicating that ExACT is following an established pattern in South African dental software.

For medical scheme pre‑authorisations, ExACT uses the same planned codes but structures them into a proposal that can be submitted electronically to the scheme via its claims switching interface, or possibly via email or fax where schemes do not support full EDI for pre‑authorisations.[9] The Health Focus article on handling medical aid claims in 2026 notes that modern systems like Eminance can prepare, validate, and submit claims directly from the software, including pre‑claims validation and real‑time responses on benefit limits and authorisations.[21][21] ExACT’s mention of treatment plan proposals to schemes implies that it integrates to similar switch platforms and uses the same claims infrastructure for pre‑authorisations as for post‑treatment claims, with the difference that the treatment items are flagged as proposed rather than rendered, and that authorisation numbers and decisions are stored back on those plan items. This in turn affects the later billing process, as the invoicing module can check whether each treatment line has an authorisation and whether claim limits might be exceeded, prompting the practice to manage patient expectations early.

### 2.4 Clinical Charting and Charting‑to‑Billing Linkage in ExACT SA

The clinical charting function is central to ExACT SA’s value proposition, described as the feature that made it South Africa’s foremost patient record system.[5] It provides a structured odontogram including plaque scores, periodontal status, and dental status, and maintains a history of completed treatments, effectively forming an electronic chart that accumulates over the patient’s lifetime.[5] Although detailed help documentation is not publicly available, the parallels with EXACT’s graphical 2D charting and other international dental systems make it possible to infer the charting‑to‑billing linkage. In EXACT by Software of Excellence, each charted treatment is a “service” entry within a course of treatment that can transition from planned to completed status, and once completed the system allows the dentist to “charge” the course so that each completed item becomes a line on an invoice.[36] ExACT SA, which integrates clinical charting with patient invoices and medical aid claims, follows the same principle: the clinical chart is not just an image but a front end to a structured table of treatment items that can be priced and invoiced once marked as completed.[9][9][9]

Behind the scenes, each treatment record in ExACT SA likely stores attributes used in both clinical and financial contexts: service code, description, tooth number, surfaces, provider, planned date, completed date, and flags for status and claimability. The marketing emphasis on periodontal and plaque status suggests that there are also specific charting templates for periodontal charting, perhaps including pocket depth, bleeding, and plaque measurements, which are clinically important but not always directly billed; these may be stored in separate tables keyed to teeth and surfaces but still linked to visits and courses of treatment.[5] For chargeable items, however, the service code and completed date are the key drivers. Once a service is completed, the billing subsystem can query all uncharged completed items for a patient, group them by date or by course of treatment, and assemble them into invoice structures that will then be used for printing, claims and ledger postings.

Because ExACT SA is designed for the South African claims environment, this charting‑to‑billing linkage must also handle detailed coding requirements such as modifiers and direct material codes described in SADA coding manuals and CPD materials.[28] Educational content on dental coding in South Africa notes that practitioners must claim for direct materials using specific codes and may only charge handling fees by adding appropriate modifiers, and that unlisted procedures are coded with special codes like 9099 accompanied by descriptive text.[28] In a well‑designed ExACT data model, such additional codes and modifiers are either separate service codes linked to the main service, or attributes on the treatment item that can be exploded into multiple billing lines when the invoice is generated. This means that the clinical chart must allow the dentist to capture both the primary service and any associated materials or modifiers, so that when the “charge” or equivalent action is applied, the financial subsystem has the necessary granularity to produce medically and legally compliant claims.

### 2.5 Invoicing, Tariff Management, and Medical Aid Claim Submission in ExACT SA

ExACT SA places billing and claims at the centre of its productivity claims, noting that it saves money and resources in key areas including patient billing, private and insurance invoices, payment receipting, medical aid claims, and treatment estimates.[9][9][9] The existence of a fees importer with scheme‑specific tariff files suggests that ExACT stores its pricing logic in one or more fee schedule tables, with each schedule corresponding to a scheme option or private price list. The importer file names such as BANKMED‑TRA or DISCOVERY map directly to South African medical aids, indicating that ExACT users can import updated tariffs from schemes and have them automatically associated with the right patients based on their scheme membership.[49] This pattern parallels how payor configuration works in EXACT by Software of Excellence, where payor records set both the service fees and the payor portion, and each service is configured to indicate how the patient and payor charges are split.[39][39] In a South African adaptation, ExACT would use scheme tariffs to populate scheme fee columns and practice fee columns, and would calculate patient co‑payments as the difference between practice fee and scheme allowed amount, possibly plus amounts for non‑covered services.[21][21][49]

Once the dentist or front desk staff initiates invoicing for completed treatment, ExACT SA assembles invoice line items from completed treatment records, applies the appropriate fee schedule based on the patient’s scheme membership or private status, and writes one or more invoice records to the patient ledger. Because South African billing often mixes scheme‑billed items and patient‑billed items on the same day, ExACT likely supports splitting invoices or generating both scheme claims and patient invoices from the same set of treatment items. Medical billing articles emphasise that modern systems streamline this process by allowing charges to be validated prior to submission, checking ICD‑10 codes, benefit limits, and scheme‑specific rules, and alerting staff to potential rejections before claims are sent.[21][21] Given ExACT’s positioning as a leading integrated billing solution, it is reasonable to infer that its invoicing subsystem has built‑in checks against tariff tables, patient eligibility data, and scheme rules, either through internal validations or via real‑time responses from switching houses.

Electronic submission of claims is then performed through ExACT’s integration with South African switching platforms, using EDI claims formats that encode patient, provider, scheme, and service‑line information in structured segments.[9][25][25] Astera’s description of EDI healthcare claims highlights that formats like ANSI X12 837 (or analogous local standards) are used for providers to communicate about services and coverage to insurers, and that such claims must be correctly formed with the right identifiers and codes to avoid rejections.[25][25] Health Focus’ discussion of Eminance indicates that a modern SA practice system prepares claims from its internal data, validates them, and submits them electronically to switching houses, which then connect to schemes; responses such as acceptance, rejections, and remittances are fed back into the system for reconciliation.[21][21] The ExACT “7 reasons” page explicitly includes “claims to medical insurance (electronic claims submissions)” among its features, meaning that ExACT must not only generate claims but also track their status and provide reconciliation features similar to those described for Eminance and GoodX.[9][21][42]

Claim reconciliation typically involves receiving electronic remittance advice (ERAs) or similar responses from schemes, parsing them, and matching each paid or rejected line item back to an invoice or claim line in the patient ledger. The GoodX article notes that leading South African medical aid billing software can submit and validate claims instantly with schemes like Discovery and Bonitas and track responses with real‑time remittance updates, thereby reducing manual work and improving cash flow.[42] While ExACT does not publicly detail its ERA handling, its emphasis on managing the entire flow of finances and digesting payments strongly suggests that it supports importing and posting remittances to patient accounts, automatically marking scheme portions as paid, and calculating patient residual balances for co‑payments or rejected items.[9][42][9] This implies entities such as Claim, ClaimLine, Remittance, and RemittanceLine in the underlying data model, linked by foreign keys to the respective invoice and invoice‑line records.

### 2.6 Statements, Reconciliation and Reporting in ExACT SA

The final stage of ExACT SA’s end‑to‑end flow is the reconciliation of patient and scheme accounts and the generation of statements and management reports. Marketing material states that ExACT handles patient invoices and statements, payment receipting, and digesting payments, and that it influences almost all financial flows in the practice, implying that it offers built‑in functions for printing or emailing statements to patients and possibly to third‑party payors.[9][9][9] Although ExACT SA’s statement interface is not publicly documented, one can infer a strong similarity to EXACT’s patient statement module, where statements are generated from the patient transaction window and can be output as printed, previewed, emailed, or posted via a service.[17][17] In EXACT, statements can be filtered by provider and configured with custom messages, and the output can be a PDF attached to an email or sent via postal services such as Easypost.[17][17] ExACT SA likely implements analogous options, given that the communication of balances to patients is a universal requirement.

Reconciliation also includes the ability to adjust transactions, correct billing errors, and manage patient balances over time. EXACT provides an “Adjust” function in the patient transactions screen that allows users to adjust invoices, typically not receipts, with specified adjustment types and reasons, and posts these adjustments as new transaction lines without altering the original record.[41] Even if ExACT SA’s UI is different, the underlying accounting principles are the same: original invoices and receipts must remain as historical records; adjustments are added as separate entries with references to the originals; and the patient balance is recalculated from the cumulative effect of all transactions.[41][44] This approach is critical for auditability, especially in environments regulated by health professional councils and tax authorities.[31]

On the reporting side, while ExACT SA’s reports module is not publicly enumerated, its position as a comprehensive solution suggests it provides management reports for production, collections, and claims, possibly including aging of accounts receivable and scheme‑level breakdowns, similar to what is described for other systems.[30][50] The Invoiced Work by Owning Dentist report in EXACT, for instance, calculates how much to pay providers who have worked on other providers’ patients and breaks down invoiced amounts by payor and private work, which is crucial for associating income to clinicians.[30] ExACT SA is used in multi‑dentist practices and likely offers similar provider‑level financial reports, as well as scheme reports that reconcile claims submitted versus paid per medical aid, which is invaluable in the South African context where multiple schemes with different benefits are billed concurrently.[42] From a data‑model perspective, this means that every invoice, claim and payment must be tagged with provider, payor, and date attributes to enable the wide variety of summary and drill‑down reports that practice owners expect.

## 3. EXACT by Software of Excellence (Henry Schein One): Architecture and Clinical–Administrative Integration

### 3.1 Overall Architecture and Module Structure in EXACT SOE

EXACT by Software of Excellence, now part of Henry Schein One, is marketed internationally as a complete practice management system that helps dental practitioners improve patient experience, automate administrative tasks, and grow their business.[4][11][13] It is described as integrating scheduling, clinical charting, billing, and patient data into a single secure platform, supporting both single‑site and multi‑provider practices.[14][14][29] The product’s lineage from Software of Excellence, founded in New Zealand in 1988, and its later inclusion in Henry Schein One’s global portfolio, underscores its maturity and evolution as a feature‑rich dental PMS.[13][13] At architecture level, EXACT can be conceptualized as a Windows‑based client–server application layered over a multi‑table relational database, with supplementary components for imaging integration, email, and online services such as patient portal and online booking.[11][6][6]

The major functional areas of EXACT are well documented in the vendor’s help centre. There is a patient file module that stores demographic details, appointments, communications, and charting in one place.[35] The appointment book module manages booking, rescheduling, and arrival workflows, including family appointments, arrival status indicators, and appointment notes that support communication between front desk and surgeries.[3][7][3][7] The clinical module provides graphical 2D tooth charting (odontogram), with base, historical and current charting layers, plus per‑tooth treatment history, and supports multiple courses of treatment per patient.[15][38][38] Treatment planning is built around the concept of a course of treatment (COT) and linked to powerful estimate generation tools that can produce standard, graphical or pictorial estimates, including embedded images, and can be signed electronically via Clinipad.[20][24][24] The billing module uses a patient transactions or ledger view for invoices, receipts, adjustments, and statements, with configurable payors and fee schedules that split payor and patient portions.[39][41][39][17] Additional modules include Care Manager (for outstanding treatment), automated recalls, patient portal, online booking, and a comprehensive reports module with financial, clinical and marketing reports.[16][32][2][2][16][29]

In terms of system integration, Henry Schein One emphasizes that EXACT integrates securely with third‑party platforms to create end‑to‑end clinical and practice workflows, including imaging, payment processing, and other tools, positioning EXACT as the central node in a connected tech stack.[6][6] This is consistent with modern PMS design where the internal data model remains practice‑centric, while external services are integrated via APIs, DICOM, HL7, or proprietary connectors as needed. For data‑model design, the important aspect is that the core entities — patients, services, courses of treatment, invoices — remain first‑class citizens, and external systems read or write to these entities via well‑structured interfaces rather than fragmenting the data.

### 3.2 Patient Record, Families and Payor Configuration in EXACT SOE

The patient record in EXACT acts as the central hub for a patient’s information, storing demographics, appointments, communication history, charting, transactions, and more in one file.[35] This file is designed not just for individual patients but also for grouping related patients into families, which is key for workflows like family appointment booking and family billing. EXACT’s “Updating family members and family information” documentation explains that patients can be grouped into families by designating one patient as the head of family and then adding other patients as family members, with family name, address and home phone auto‑populated from the head of family when new members are created.[22][22][22] The head of family can be changed at any time by ticking a box next to the appropriate family member, and existing patients can be joined to a family through the patient file interface, enabling flexible configuration as family circumstances change.[22][22][22]

This family grouping is used to simplify both appointment and billing workflows. For appointments, EXACT allows booking of family appointments where multiple family members are scheduled together using tools that rely on the family relationships defined in their patient records; the software supports adding family members to appointments and adjusting who attends a particular time slot, aiming to maximize convenience for families and practice efficiency.[7][7][22] For billing, the head‑of‑family concept implies that the head can be designated as the party who pays, functioning similarly to a guarantor, which aligns with general patterns in dental practice management where one responsible party handles bills for multiple related patients.[22][22][44][22] Carestream Sensei’s ledger documentation, for example, includes explicit notions of “Guarantor For” and “Guaranteed By”, showing that this pattern is widely used, and EXACT’s family module provides the relational underpinnings for such financial groupings.[44]

Payor configuration in EXACT acts as the counterpart to ExACT SA’s medical aid membership module. The help article on payor configuration explains that payor records set both the service fees and the payor’s portion for each service, with each service manually configured to indicate how the patient and payor charges should be calculated.[39][39] This means that the data model includes a Payor entity with attributes like name, fee calculation method, and possibly plan rules, and a join between services and payors where fees and splits are stored. When a course of treatment is created for a patient, EXACT prompts the user to confirm the payor, which defaults from the patient record but can be changed per COT, ensuring that the right fee schedule and payor–patient split are applied to all services within that course.[39] While in UK and ANZ this is often used for private fee tables, capitation plans and schemes like the NHS, the pattern is directly translatable to South African medical aids where each scheme and option could be modelled as a payor in EXACT, with payor‑specific fee schedules and patient co‑payment logic.[39][39]

### 3.3 Appointment Book, Patient Arrival and Appointment Notes

The appointment book in EXACT is a core front‑office module that manages the day‑to‑day schedule. It supports viewing providers’ schedules, booking and rescheduling appointments, and marking patients as arrived, with visual indicators such as “stickman” icons that signal arrival status to providers.[29] When a patient or family arrives and is “accepted into the practice” by ticking the checkbox in the appointment book, the system opens an Arrival screen that shows patient details and, if the practice uses Patient Portal, the status of pre‑appointment forms. Staff can use this screen to check whether medical history or other forms have been completed online and can resend forms to Clinipad or print them out, ensuring consent and medical history are up to date before the patient is called into surgery. After all tasks are completed, clicking Arrive closes the window and displays a stick figure on the appointment book for that appointment, and only the provider whose appointment the patient is booked with will see the stickman on their screen, providing a tight coupling between appointment status and clinical workflow.

Appointment notes provide a structured way to pass information between reception and clinical teams. EXACT’s help centre notes that appointment notes can be added either via the edit appointment window or via the chart screen when setting up planned treatment appointments, and that they are particularly useful for highlighting information that the practice should know, such as specific preparation required, patient preferences, or statements about previous no‑shows.[3][3] Notes added in the surgeries can be seen by reception when planned appointments appear on the depart bar, ensuring that both sides see relevant context.[3][3] Technically, this means that the Appointment entity has associated notes records, which can be authored in different contexts but are stored in a unified table keyed by appointment ID. For the purposes of a data model, this illustrates that even seemingly simple functions like notes must be relationally linked to their parent entities, with metadata about author, timestamp and source.

Family appointments are supported directly in the appointment book. A training video explains that once family members have been linked in the patient record, users can book family appointments by selecting one member, using the family appointment tool to choose which family members will attend, adjusting appointment length, and then moving the combined appointment into the appointment book, either via drag‑and‑drop or using a search facility for suitable times.[7][7] Behind this UI, EXACT must create either separate appointment records for each family member linked to a family appointment group, or a single appointment record with multiple patient links, depending on design. In either case, the family entity and its relationships to patients are critical, demonstrating how the patient account structure influences appointment scheduling workflows.

### 3.4 Clinical Charting, Odontogram and Treatment History

EXACT’s clinical charting module uses a graphical 2D odontogram representing the patient’s upper and lower jaws and supports permanent, mixed and deciduous dentitions.[38][38] Users can toggle between permanent, mixed, and deciduous tooth charts using a P/D switch, and practice settings control the default for new patients, which is particularly important for paediatric patients who may move from deciduous to mixed to permanent dentitions over time.[38] The chart can be viewed in different modes such as base charting only, all charting, or current charting, where base charting represents long‑term status, historical charting shows past treatments, and current charting shows treatments being added in open courses of treatment.[38][38] By hovering over a tooth, users can display charting for that specific tooth, including historical and current treatments, and access a quick chart menu with tooth‑specific options.[38][38]

Treatment history is accessible via a History button (H) in the Chart tab, which opens a window showing the patient’s historical and current treatment, including dates, providers, graphical representations, service codes including tooth and surfaces, line‑item particulars, total fee, patient and payor portions, and any clinical notes or invoice notes attached to services.[15] This window allows filtering of failed and voided treatments, and displays treatment sorted by date, tooth number, or plan, and grouped into base chart items, historical chart items, and current uncharged treatment items.[15] Users can search within this history by service names, notes, custom screen fields, and provider codes, and view per‑tooth history by double‑clicking above or below a tooth in the chart or using the Quick Chart Menu.[15] Voiding historical treatment items is supported, with voided treatments remaining in history but hidden by default, and accessible by checking Show Void, which is important for preserving audit trails without cluttering the chart.[15]

From a data‑model perspective, this means that EXACT maintains one or more Treatment tables keyed by patient, course of treatment, and tooth, with attributes for service code, status (planned, completed, failed, voided), and financial fields for fee and payor/patient splits.[15] Links to clinical notes, invoice notes, custom screens, and x‑ray images are stored either as foreign keys or as join tables to document and imaging tables; for example, x‑rays can be viewed via an Examine Pro integration, and icons in the history window indicate attached x‑rays, notes, or custom screens.[15][38] The voiding mechanism implies that treatment records are never deleted once charged; instead, their status is updated and a “voided” flag toggled, and the UI decides whether to display them. This pattern is important to emulate in any custom system, to ensure medico‑legal traceability of treatment history.

### 3.5 Treatment Planning, Courses of Treatment, Estimates and Care Manager

Treatment planning in EXACT revolves around the concept of the course of treatment (COT). To chart treatment, users must first create a COT from within the patient record’s Chart tab; the system then prompts for payor, number of appointments, and allows editing of description and charting colours, with payor defaulting from the patient record. Once created, the COT appears in the treatment area as a tab identified by a COT number, providing a distinct context for charted services. Within this COT, users add treatment services either via quick charting, quick plans, or by selecting individual services from a service list, then clicking on appropriate teeth for tooth‑specific items or spanning teeth for bridges and other multi‑tooth treatments. Charted services store planned and completed dates, estimated and actual time, provider, tooth number, fee, and notes, and can be edited while uncharged; once charged, editing requires resubmitting the COT as described in a training video on resubmitting treatments.[37]

Treatment plan estimates are a separate but closely related feature. EXACT allows users to generate treatment plan estimates from the Chart tab by clicking an estimate button, selecting whether to include all open treatment or individual treatment plans, choosing the date and provider, and selecting whether the estimate is for all courses of treatment, the current course, or highlighted treatment lines only.[24][24] After these selections, a Print Standard Estimate window offers choices between Standard, Graphical, or Pictorial estimates, with regional options such as NHS, and users can then choose to print, preview, email, or send the estimate to Clinipad for electronic signature.[24][24] Pictorial estimates can include images from intra‑oral cameras, digital cameras, or scanned x‑rays, selected via a dialog that allows multiple image selection, enhancing patient understanding.[24][24] All created estimates are stored on the patient file as PDF documents in an Estimates tab, where they can be viewed, printed, or emailed later, and each estimate shows icons indicating whether it has been printed, emailed, or signed via Clinipad.[24][24] This indicates that the data model includes an Estimate entity linked to one or more treatment items (often by COT and service IDs) and to a file storage mechanism for associated PDFs.

Care Manager, introduced in training videos, is an integrated feature that allows staff to view, book, and assign outstanding treatments by listing patients with open courses of treatment and no appointments booked.[32][32][32] It displays patient names, provider initials, payor or payor area, course of treatment numbers, last appointment dates, and status, and provides quick access to patient details for booking or follow‑up.[32][32] Users can book patients directly from Care Manager, filter by provider, assign patients to specific users for follow‑up, and snooze or reject patients with reasons, enabling targeted recall of incomplete treatments.[32][32] From a data perspective, Care Manager operates on query logic over the treatment and appointment tables: it selects COTs with outstanding treatment and no future appointments, and surfaces them as tasks. This pattern is valuable for custom system design because it shows how longitudinal treatment and scheduling data can be combined into actionable queues that drive production.

### 3.6 Charging, Invoices, Transactions, Statements and Scheme Reporting

The transition from treatment planning to billing in EXACT occurs through the “Charge” operation on courses of treatment. After charting and marking treatments as completed, users press the Charge button, which posts the treatment items as charges, generating invoices in the patient transactions screen.[36][36] Documentation notes that once treatments have been ticked or completed, the Charge button triggers the financial posting, and that if corrections are needed after charging, users can resubmit the COT, adjust invoices, and recharge as necessary, ensuring a clear audit trail.[36][37][36] The patient transactions screen, accessible via a currency symbol button, lists invoices, receipts, and adjustments, and supports adjustments to invoices via an Adjust button, where users choose adjustment types, enter reasons, and specify amounts, with the system posting new adjustment lines rather than overwriting the original transactions.[41] Adjustments are then visible in lists of all adjustments accessed via Transactions → List Items → List Adjustments.[41]

Patient statements in EXACT are generated from the patient transactions window by selecting a patient and then Statement, which opens a Print Statement window with options to include all providers or filter to one or multiple providers.[17][17] Users can edit messages on the statements and then choose how to output the statement: printing to a selected printer, previewing on screen with optional printing, emailing as a PDF attachment to the patient’s email, or sending via Easypost for postal delivery.[17][17] This flexibility shows that the Statement entity is not stored as a persistent document by default but is generated on demand from ledger data, though some practices may store PDFs for their own records. The presence of statement runs for printing multiple statements suggests that statements can also be generated in batch using queries, mirroring typical accounts‑receivable workflows.[17][17][50]

Payor configuration and scheme reporting are central to EXACT’s ability to handle third‑party payors. Payor records, as noted earlier, define how fees are calculated and how the payor and patient portions are allocated for each service, with service definitions including code, description, chart graphic, colour, and fee tables per price list.[39][47][48][39] The reports module offers a variety of reporting options grouped by patient accounts, invoices, payments, marketing, practice management, clinical management, and schemes, indicating that scheme‑specific reporting is first‑class.[2][2][2][2] For example, the Invoiced Work by Owning Dentist report shows for each provider the amount of treatment that has been invoiced, divided into payor and private work, and separated into work done on their own patients and on other providers’ patients.[30] Reports can be filtered by provider, provider category, date ranges, and whether to show zero‑value invoices or stock items, and can be previewed, printed, or exported for spreadsheet analysis.[30][2] The presence of a “Schemes” report group implies that EXACT can report on scheme contributions, patient co‑payments, and outstanding scheme balances, which is the analogue of medical aid reporting in ExACT SA.[2][2][2]

### 3.7 Recalls, Patient Portal, Online Booking and Reporting

EXACT incorporates sophisticated patient engagement tools around recalls and online services. Automated recalls are designed to send reminders to patients about due or overdue check‑ups, hygiene appointments, or other recall‑type visits, without manual input from staff.[16][16] The software allows practices to configure recall strategies so that patients receive reminders exactly when they need them, via channels such as SMS, email, or letters, and tracks recall status so that staff can focus on exceptions rather than routine reminders.[16][16] This functionality depends on recall entities linked to patients, with attributes such as due date, type, last reminder date, and status, and on integration with the communication subsystem that manages message templates and delivery logs.

Patient Portal and online booking extend this engagement into self‑service. Henry Schein One’s blog on online booking and patient portal changes notes that EXACT’s Online Booking has been updated to provide simplified booking in two steps, mobile‑friendly responsive design, and improved confirmation flows. The patient portal is also used in the arrivals workflow; when a patient is arrived, staff can check whether pre‑appointment forms have been completed via Patient Portal, and can resend forms to Clinipad or print them, integrating online and in‑practice workflows. From a data‑model standpoint, this means that forms are stored as structured or semi‑structured data linked to patients and appointments, with statuses that can be checked programmatically and displayed in both portal and in‑practice UI. The portal and online booking modules read from and write to the same appointment and patient tables as the desktop application, ensuring consistency.

Reporting in EXACT is comprehensive. The reports module, accessible via Administration → Reports or a workspace icon, lists all available reports and allows filtering by group, with groups of Patient Accounts, Invoices, Payments, Marketing, Practice Management, Clinical Management, and Schemes.[2][2][2][2] Users can narrow the list of reports via filters, select queries to restrict reports to specific patient segments, and choose output formats such as on‑screen preview, print, and export.[2][2][2] These capabilities imply that EXACT’s database includes not only transactional tables but also views, stored procedures, or reporting layers that aggregate data for common metrics, such as monthly production, collections, case acceptance, and recall effectiveness, consistent with how PMS products emphasize analytics.[29] For the design of a custom system, this highlights the importance of capturing sufficient metadata (provider, payor, service type, referral source, etc.) at the transactional level so that meaningful analytics can be derived later.

## 4. Cross‑System Data Model and Workflow Patterns

### 4.1 Core Entities and Relationships: A Shared Conceptual Schema

Despite differences in scope and regional focus, both ExACT SA and EXACT SOE share a core set of entities and relationships that underpin their workflows. At the centre is the Patient entity, representing an individual receiving care, with attributes such as name, date of birth, contact details, and identifiers.[35][5] Patients can belong to a Family or Account entity representing a group of related patients for scheduling and billing, where one member is designated as the head of family or guarantor.[22][22][44][22] In South Africa, this family concept must be tightly linked to medical aid membership, with a MainMember entity associated with one or more Dependants, each of whom may also be a Patient in the system.[21]

Each patient has zero or more Appointments, representing scheduled visits to the practice, with links to Provider entities (dentists or hygienists) and possibly to Rooms or Chairs.[7][7][29] When a patient is examined and treatment is planned, the system creates a CourseOfTreatment (COT) or similar plan entity linked to the patient and payor, containing multiple TreatmentItems.[5] Each TreatmentItem references a Service (procedure) entity, which has attributes such as code, description, chart graphic, and pricing details per fee schedule.[47][48][49][5] Services should be mapped to standard coding systems (such as local dental codes, HPCSA guideline codes, or scheme codes), enabling claims generation and reporting.[31]

FeeSchedule entities define prices for services per payor or plan; for example, a private fee schedule and separate schedules for each medical aid and option imported via ExACT’s fees importer or configured via EXACT’s payor fee settings.[39][39][49] Invoices are built from completed TreatmentItems, with InvoiceLine entities referencing both the invoice and the underlying treatment records, and storing the chosen fee, patient portion, and payor portion.[36][41][9] Payments are recorded as Payment entities linked to patients, invoices, and optionally payors, with PaymentLines allocating amounts to specific invoices or accounts, and Adjustments represent corrections or discounts applied after the fact.[41][44][9] Claims and ClaimLines represent submissions to third‑party payors, referencing invoice lines, and Remittances and RemittanceLines represent responses from payors, matched back to claim lines and invoice lines.[21][21][25][25]

In addition, both systems maintain Statements, which are essentially formatted views over ledger entries for a patient or family, and Reports, which are either views or aggregated datasets for analytics.[17][17][2][2][2] Recalls or RecallItems link patients to due dates for preventive care; Communication records link patients and possibly appointments to emails, SMSs, letters and forms; and Estimate entities link treatment items to patient‑facing or payor‑facing quotation documents.[16][24][24][16] Finally, there are AuditTrail entities, either explicit or implicit, to record who created or modified clinical and financial records, especially when resubmitting treatments or voiding items.[15][37][41]

From a relational design standpoint, these entities can be captured in an ERD where each table has a primary key and foreign keys to related tables, as described in ERD tutorials that emphasize unique, never‑changing primary keys and foreign keys that enforce relationships.[33] For example, PatientID is the primary key in the Patient table; FamilyID is the primary key in the Family table, and Patient records have a nullable FamilyID foreign key; CourseOfTreatment records have PatientID and PayorID foreign keys; TreatmentItems have COTID, ServiceID, ToothID and ProviderID; and InvoiceLines have InvoiceID and TreatmentItemID, enabling traceability from each invoice line back to a clinical procedure.

### 4.2 Patient–Family–Payor and Main Member–Dependant Relationships

One of the more subtle but critical design aspects is the relationship between patients, families, and payors. EXACT illustrates how a flexible family grouping can support both scheduling and billing by allowing any patient to be head of family and by enabling existing patients to be added or removed from families as circumstances change.[22][22][22] It also gives practices the ability to set appointment and mailing preferences at the family level, which can simplify recall and communication workflows.[22][22] Carestream’s ledger concept of guarantors reinforces the pattern of separating the recipient of care from the party financially responsible for the account.[44] ExACT SA must go further by modelling the main medical aid member and dependants, because schemes expect claims to be submitted under the main member’s ID and membership, with dependants coded via beneficiary numbers.[21]

A robust custom data model for a South African dental practice should therefore distinguish between at least three related constructs: a Patient, representing the clinical subject; a BillingAccount or Family, representing the unit of financial responsibility; and a SchemeMembership, representing the contract between a person (usually the main member) and a medical aid. Each BillingAccount can have one or more Patients, with one designated as GuarantorPatientID or an external ResponsibleParty entity; each Patient can have zero or more SchemeMembershipLinks pointing to a SchemeMembership entity and indicating the role (MainMember or Dependant) and the dependant number.[21][21][44] SchemeMembership holds scheme code, plan, main member ID and effective dates; by linking dependants to the same membership, claims can reference the correct membership details for each patient.

This separation allows modelling situations where a guardian who is not the main scheme member pays for a child’s co‑payments, or where a patient has dual coverage. It also aligns with EXACT’s payor configuration, where the payor (scheme or plan) is specified at the COT or treatment level, but the head of family or guarantor is specified at the account level for patient‑balance billing.[39][39][17] In ExACT SA, treatment plan proposals and claims would use the SchemeMembership’s main member data, while patient statements would be grouped by BillingAccount, ensuring that the right person receives the account irrespective of scheme relationships.[21][9][9][9]

### 4.3 Course‑of‑Treatment‑Centric Flow: Linking Charting to Billing

A striking commonality between the two systems is their course‑of‑treatment‑centric view of clinical and financial flow. EXACT makes this explicit: to chart treatment users create a course of treatment, within which they add services, track planned versus completed dates, and then “charge” completed services to generate invoices.[36] The course of treatment thus functions as both a clinical grouping (related treatments forming a coherent episode of care) and a financial control (determining which services are batched into a charge event). Care Manager in EXACT uses open courses of treatment with unbooked appointments as the basis for follow‑up, further emphasizing the centrality of COTs.[32][32]

ExACT SA, while less explicitly documented, almost certainly uses a similar concept. Its clinical charting screen and completed treatment history provide the clinical side of the picture, and its treatment estimates, plan proposals and claims require groupings of related treatment items to generate meaningful documents.[9][9][5] Given its integration of charting, billing and claims, the natural pivot is a plan or course entity that groups treatment items and carries payor and authorisation context, similar to EXACT’s COT. This entity would have attributes like description (for example, “Upper left quadrant rehabilitation”), payor (scheme or private), start and end dates, status, and possibly authorisation number. It would be linked to multiple treatment items; these, once completed, would be transformed into invoice lines during the charging process.[9][5]

In a custom data model, designing such a CourseOfTreatment entity as a central bridge between clinical and financial tables offers several advantages. It allows multiple COTs per patient, which is common when patients have parallel episodes such as orthodontics and restorative care. It allows different payors per COT, which is important when some treatments are covered by schemes and others are cosmetic and self‑pay. It provides a unit of analysis for reports about case acceptance and production per treatment plan.[30] It also simplifies audit trails, since each treatment item’s pathway from planned to completed to invoiced can be traced within the context of its COT.

### 4.4 Fee Schedules, Tariff Imports, and Price Calculation

Both ExACT SA and EXACT SOE rely on structured fee schedules to price services, but they differ in the degree of automation and regulatory coupling. EXACT’s payor configuration defines fee calculation per payor, and service definitions include fee tables per price list, with configuration determining whether patient and payor charges are taken from payor‑specific fee tables or from a standard table.[39][47][39] This allows practices to define separate price lists for private pay, capitation plans, and schemes like NHS, and to control which fee list applies per patient or COT. The help articles suggest that fees can be configured per service and payor, and that patient and payor portions can be set to calculated or fixed percentages, providing flexibility for different plan designs.[39][39]

ExACT SA introduces additional sophistication via its fees importer, which provides pre‑built fee tables for numerous medical aids and plan options such as BankMed Traditional and Discovery variants, packaged as importable data.[49] By importing these tariffs, practices can ensure their scheme fee schedules match the latest scheme rates and possibly cover benefits and limits. HPCSA’s guideline tariffs and Council for Medical Schemes standardisation work also influence fee schedules, providing recommended charges and mapping codes to services, although schemes often pay at their own rates.[31] In practice, ExACT SA likely stores for each service and scheme two or more fee values: the scheme allowed amount, the practice’s desired fee, and the patient co‑payment (derived). Because South African schemes may limit benefits for certain codes or require pre‑authorisations, the fee schedule module may also store flags indicating whether a code requires authorisation or is excluded from benefits for particular plans.[21][21][42]

A custom system can adopt a generic FeeSchedule and FeeScheduleItem design, where FeeSchedule records represent combinations of payor and plan (for example, “DISCOVERY CLASSIC 2026” or “PRIVATE 2026”), and FeeScheduleItem records link each schedule to a ServiceID and provide fields for AllowedAmount, PracticeFee, PatientPortion, and PayorPortion. Calculation logic in the billing module then determines, for each invoice line, which fee schedule to use (usually based on the patient’s active SchemeMembership and the COT’s payor) and how to compute patient co‑payments and payor claims. For example, PatientPortion could be computed as PracticeFee minus AllowedAmount for covered codes, or simply PracticeFee if the code is excluded, while PayorPortion would be AllowedAmount if the service is covered.[21][21][49] This structure matches both EXACT’s payor fee concept and ExACT SA’s tariff imports, while remaining flexible enough for non‑South‑African settings.

### 4.5 Claims, Remittances and Statements: Revenue Cycle Patterns

At the end of the clinical–financial chain lies the revenue cycle, encompassing claims submission, remittance processing, and statements. Astera’s guide to EDI claims notes that electronic claims submitted using standardized formats such as ANSI X12 EDI 837 facilitate efficient communication between providers and insurers about coverage and reimbursement.[25][25] South African practice systems like Eminance and GoodX mirror this pattern in local standards: they prepare claims from internal data, integrate with switching houses, perform real‑time validation, submit claims electronically, and process responses such as approvals, rejections, and remittances, with the goal of reducing manual intervention and improving cash flow.[21][21][42] ExACT SA explicitly supports claims to medical insurance via electronic claim submissions and treatment plan proposals to schemes, placing it firmly in this modern revenue‑cycle framework.[9][9]

EXACT by Software of Excellence, while less focused on medical schemes in public documentation, supports scheme reporting and payor configuration, and in markets like the UK integrates with NHS claims workflows which also involve electronic submissions and remittances.[2][2][39][2] Its ledger and adjustment mechanisms shed light on general patterns: original charges are posted as invoices, payments are posted separately and matched to invoices, and adjustments are posted to correct errors or apply write‑offs, preserving the integrity of the original documents.[41][44] Patient statements aggregate transactions by patient or guarantor over a period, showing opening balance, charges, payments, adjustments, and closing balance.[17][17] For insurance payments, ledger lines include payer names, claim numbers, and dates of service, providing direct linkage between payments and claims, as seen in Sensei’s ledger documentation.[44]

A custom system can therefore model claims and remittances using generic EDI‑oriented entities. A Claim entity represents a submission batch to a single payor, with attributes such as payor, submission date, and status; ClaimLine entities link claim records to InvoiceLines, storing claim‑specific data such as EDI line numbers, billed amount, and service dates.[25][25] When remittances are received (either electronically as ERAs or manually captured), Remittance entities record high‑level information such as payor, remittance date, and total paid, and RemittanceLine entities link remittances to ClaimLines and InvoiceLines, capturing allowed amounts, paid amounts, write‑offs, and denial codes.[21][21][25] Posting remittances then updates Payment and Adjustment records in the patient ledger to reflect scheme payments and residual patient balances. Statements can be generated using queries across the ledger, summarizing charges and payments, and optionally showing claim statuses for transparency about what the scheme has paid versus what the patient owes.[17][17][42]

## 5. Designing a Custom South African Dental Practice System: Applying the Patterns

### 5.1 Architectural Considerations for a Single‑Practice System

For a single South African dental practice, the architectural decisions are less about multi‑site scalability and more about robustness, maintainability, and integration with local medical aid infrastructure. HealthCluster’s discussion of medical practice management software in South Africa emphasizes core features such as fast check‑ins, accurate billing, and improved patient satisfaction, underpinned by coherent integration between front office, clinical and billing functions. Modern PMS products often adopt a cloud‑based architecture to simplify updates and enable access from multiple locations, but many South African practices still use on‑premise client‑server systems due to connectivity constraints and legacy integrations.[21][42] In either architecture, the data model remains relational, and the subsystems mirror those seen in ExACT and EXACT.

A reasonable approach is to design a central relational database that holds all operational data, a business logic layer that implements workflows for patient registration, charting, billing and claims, and a user interface layer that may be web‑based, desktop, or hybrid. Integration with SA switching houses and medical aid billing platforms requires either direct EDI connections or use of third‑party connectors similar to how iDentalSoft integrates with clearinghouses for e‑claims processing, batch submissions, and ERA tracking.[40][40][40] Designing the system as an “integration hub” aligns with the Oryx concept of PMS as the home base to which imaging devices, payment processors, and other tools connect, ensuring that all clinical and financial data remains in one coherent repository.

### 5.2 Example Database Schema for Key Entities

To make the abstractions concrete, the following simplified SQL schema illustrates how core entities discussed above could be defined, drawing on ERD principles of primary keys and foreign keys as described in database design tutorials.[33] This is not exhaustive but provides a foundation for modelling patients, families, scheme memberships, courses of treatment, services, invoices and claims.

```sql
CREATE TABLE Patient (
    PatientID          INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    FamilyID           INT NULL,
    FirstName          NVARCHAR(100),
    LastName           NVARCHAR(100),
    DateOfBirth        DATE,
    SouthAfricanID     NVARCHAR(20) NULL,
    MobilePhone        NVARCHAR(30) NULL,
    Email              NVARCHAR(200) NULL,
    IsActive           BIT NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,
    CONSTRAINT FK_Patient_Family FOREIGN KEY (FamilyID)
        REFERENCES Family(FamilyID)
);

CREATE TABLE Family (
    FamilyID           INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    HeadPatientID      INT NULL,
    BillingName        NVARCHAR(200),
    AddressLine1       NVARCHAR(200),
    AddressLine2       NVARCHAR(200) NULL,
    City               NVARCHAR(100),
    PostalCode         NVARCHAR(20),
    CONSTRAINT FK_Family_HeadPatient FOREIGN KEY (HeadPatientID)
        REFERENCES Patient(PatientID)
);

CREATE TABLE Scheme (
    SchemeID           INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    SchemeCode         NVARCHAR(50) UNIQUE,
    Name               NVARCHAR(200),
    Administrator      NVARCHAR(200) NULL
);

CREATE TABLE SchemeMembership (
    MembershipID       INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    SchemeID           INT NOT NULL,
    MainMemberPatientID INT NOT NULL,
    MemberNumber       NVARCHAR(50),
    OptionCode         NVARCHAR(50),
    EffectiveFrom      DATE,
    EffectiveTo        DATE NULL,
    CONSTRAINT FK_Membership_Scheme FOREIGN KEY (SchemeID)
        REFERENCES Scheme(SchemeID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_Membership_MainMember FOREIGN KEY (MainMemberPatientID)
        REFERENCES Patient(PatientID)
);

CREATE TABLE MembershipDependant (
    MembershipID       INT NOT NULL,
    PatientID          INT NOT NULL,
    DependantCode      NVARCHAR(10),
    PRIMARY KEY (MembershipID, PatientID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_MDep_Membership FOREIGN KEY (MembershipID)
        REFERENCES SchemeMembership(MembershipID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_MDep_Patient FOREIGN KEY (PatientID)
        REFERENCES Patient(PatientID)
);

CREATE TABLE Service (
    ServiceID          INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    InternalCode       NVARCHAR(50) UNIQUE,
    Description        NVARCHAR(200),
    Hpcsacode          NVARCHAR(50) NULL,
    ToothSpecific      BIT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0
);

CREATE TABLE FeeSchedule (
    FeeScheduleID      INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    Name               NVARCHAR(200),
    SchemeID           INT NULL,
    OptionCode         NVARCHAR(50) NULL,
    Year               INT,
    CONSTRAINT FK_FeeSchedule_Scheme FOREIGN KEY (SchemeID)
        REFERENCES Scheme(SchemeID)
);

CREATE TABLE FeeScheduleItem (
    FeeScheduleID      INT NOT NULL,
    ServiceID          INT NOT NULL,
    AllowedAmount      DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    PracticeFee        DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    PRIMARY KEY (FeeScheduleID, ServiceID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_FeeItem_FeeSchedule FOREIGN KEY (FeeScheduleID)
        REFERENCES FeeSchedule(FeeScheduleID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_FeeItem_Service FOREIGN KEY (ServiceID)
        REFERENCES Service(ServiceID)
);

CREATE TABLE CourseOfTreatment (
    COTID              INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    PatientID          INT NOT NULL,
    PayorMembershipID  INT NULL,
    Description        NVARCHAR(200),
    Status             NVARCHAR(20),  -- Planned, Active, Completed, Closed
    StartDate          DATE,
    EndDate            DATE NULL,
    AuthorizationNumber NVARCHAR(50) NULL,
    CONSTRAINT FK_COT_Patient FOREIGN KEY (PatientID)
        REFERENCES Patient(PatientID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_COT_Membership FOREIGN KEY (PayorMembershipID)
        REFERENCES SchemeMembership(MembershipID)
);

CREATE TABLE TreatmentItem (
    TreatmentItemID    INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    COTID              INT NOT NULL,
    ServiceID          INT NOT NULL,
    ProviderID         INT NOT NULL,
    ToothNumber        NVARCHAR(10) NULL,
    Surface            NVARCHAR(5) NULL,
    PlannedDate        DATE NULL,
    CompletedDate      DATE NULL,
    Status             NVARCHAR(20),  -- Planned, Completed, Failed, Voided
    EstimatedMinutes   INT NULL,
    FeeScheduleID      INT NULL,
    PracticeFee        DECIMAL(10,2) NULL,
    CONSTRAINT FK_TI_COT FOREIGN KEY (COTID)
        REFERENCES CourseOfTreatment(COTID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_TI_Service FOREIGN KEY (ServiceID)
        REFERENCES Service(ServiceID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_TI_FeeSchedule FOREIGN KEY (FeeScheduleID)
        REFERENCES FeeSchedule(FeeScheduleID)
);

CREATE TABLE Invoice (
    InvoiceID          INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    PatientID          INT NOT NULL,
    BillingFamilyID    INT NULL,
    InvoiceDate        DATE NOT NULL,
    TotalAmount        DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    Status             NVARCHAR(20),  -- Open, PartPaid, Paid, WrittenOff
    CONSTRAINT FK_Inv_Patient FOREIGN KEY (PatientID)
        REFERENCES Patient(PatientID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_Inv_Family FOREIGN KEY (BillingFamilyID)
        REFERENCES Family(FamilyID)
);

CREATE TABLE InvoiceLine (
    InvoiceLineID      INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    InvoiceID          INT NOT NULL,
    TreatmentItemID    INT NOT NULL,
    BilledAmount       DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    PatientPortion     DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    PayorPortion       DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
    CONSTRAINT FK_IL_Invoice FOREIGN KEY (InvoiceID)
        REFERENCES Invoice(InvoiceID),
    CONSTRAINT FK_IL_Treatment FOREIGN KEY (TreatmentItemID)
        REFERENCES TreatmentItem(TreatmentItemID)
);
```

This schema borrows the COT and TreatmentItem relationship from EXACT, the Scheme and SchemeMembership patterns from ExACT SA and South African billing practices, and establishes foreign keys between clinical and financial entities in a way that supports auditability.[21][49][9][36] Additional tables for Appointment, Claim, Remittance, Payment, Adjustment, Estimate, Recall, and Communication would further flesh out the system, following the conceptual patterns discussed earlier.

### 5.3 End‑to‑End Workflow Design: Mapping Requirements 1–5

Using the combined insights from ExACT SA and EXACT SOE, the custom system’s workflows can be articulated in the same five stages the question highlights, each supported by specific entities and relationships.

For patient record and account structure, when a new patient is registered, staff capture demographics and, if relevant, associate the patient to a Family record, designating a head of family who will serve as the default billing contact, mirroring EXACT’s family setup and ExACT’s likely use of main member and guardian relationships.[22][22][22][5] If the patient belongs to a medical aid, staff create or select an existing SchemeMembership for the main member, add the patient as a dependant with the appropriate dependant code, and mark this membership as active for the patient, similar to how SA systems like Eminance and GoodX link patients to schemes for claims.[21][21][42] The patient record thus holds both the clinical identity and links to financial responsibilities and scheme coverage, allowing all subsequent modules to determine correct billing and claim behaviour.

In treatment planning and estimates, during examination the dentist uses a clinical charting interface modelled on EXACT’s odontogram and ExACT’s charting features to record findings and plan treatment by adding TreatmentItems within a CourseOfTreatment, associating each with a Service code, tooth, and surfaces.[38][5] The system computes provisional fees for each TreatmentItem by applying the appropriate FeeSchedule based on the patient’s active SchemeMembership and the COT’s payor, calculating expected scheme payments and patient co‑payments.[39][39][49] A patient‑facing Estimate is then generated, summarising planned treatments, fees, and cost splits, akin to EXACT’s treatment plan estimates and ExACT’s treatment estimates or quotations.[24][24][9][9] If necessary, a scheme‑facing pre‑authorisation request is assembled from the same TreatmentItems and submitted electronically via EDI, with authorisation numbers stored back on the COT and items upon approval.[21][25][21][25]

When treatment is performed and charted as completed, the dentist updates TreatmentItems with completed dates and status, possibly during or after each appointment, just as EXACT allows editing of charted services prior to charging and tracks planned versus completed dates.[15] Charting captures not only the services but also updated base and historical charting, periodontal measurements and plaque scores, but only chargeable items are marked for billing, paralleling ExACT SA’s clinical charting and EXACT’s separation of clinical and financial aspects.[15][38][5] Once a set of treatments is ready to bill (perhaps at the end of a visit or when a COT is clinically concluded), the system groups completed TreatmentItems and generates Invoice and InvoiceLine records by applying the fee schedules, representing the charting‑to‑billing linkage seen in both Exact products.[36][9][36]

In invoicing and billing, the system determines whether each invoice line should be billed to a scheme, the patient, or both, depending on coverage and benefit status, echoing how ExACT SA and other SA systems handle private versus medical‑aid billing.[21][9][21][9] For scheme‑covered portions, claim data is prepared and queued for submission; for patient portions, the system can request payment at checkout (cash, card or electronic) using either third‑party payment processors or embedded payment platforms, similar to how modern dental PMS integrate payments into the workflow.[40] Exact‑like patient transaction screens allow staff to view invoices, receipts and adjustments, apply discounts via adjustments when appropriate, and generate statements that show outstanding balances, drawing on EXACT’s ledger and statements model.[41][17][29]

Finally, in claim submission and reconciliation, claim batches are submitted electronically to switching houses in EDI formats, each ClaimLine referencing its InvoiceLine and storing claim identifiers.[21][25][25][9] Responses from schemes, including remittances and rejections, are imported and matched to ClaimLines and InvoiceLines, with RemittanceLines recording allowed amounts, paid amounts, and denial reasons, following the pattern described for Eminance and GoodX and implied by ExACT SA’s electronic claims features.[21][21][42][9] Posting remittances generates Payment and Adjustment records in the patient ledger, marking payor portions as paid and adjusting balances, while patient statements periodically communicate residual balances to patients or heads of family, similar to EXACT’s statement runs.[17][17][50] Reporting modules provide scheme‑level and provider‑level summaries of production, collections, write‑offs, and outstanding balances, using aggregated views over the transactional tables, comparable to EXACT’s reports grouped by patient accounts, invoices, payments, practice management and schemes.[2][30][2][2][2]

### 5.4 South African Regulatory and Coding Nuances

Designing for South Africa requires careful alignment with local regulatory and coding frameworks. HPCSA guideline tariffs for medical practitioners and dentists provide a baseline list of dental services and recommended fees, with codes such as those for prophylaxis and other procedures.[31] The Council for Medical Schemes’ standardisation work shows how unassigned CMS codes were adapted for South Africa, and emphasizes the importance of standardized coding for fair reimbursement and transparency. Educational material from SADA and the South African Dental Association stresses correct use of codes for direct materials, handling fees and unlisted procedures, with specific codes such as 8025 for handling fees and 9099 for unlisted procedures, and insists that practitioners may not mark up materials beyond invoiced costs.[28] The custom system’s Service catalog and coding subsystem must therefore support not only standard dental procedure codes but also additional direct material codes and modifiers, with validation logic to ensure compliance.

Medical aid claims handling in South Africa is increasingly automated, with tools like Eminance and GoodX integrating with electronic switch platforms for real‑time claim validation and submission.[21][21][42] Practices can submit claims to major schemes like Discovery, Bonitas and GEMS, receive immediate responses on eligibility, benefit limits and errors, and handle remittances with real‑time updates.[21][21][42] The custom system must therefore be able to produce claims in the formats expected by local switching houses, map internal codes to scheme codes, and store claim and remittance identifiers for reconciliation. It must also accommodate ICD‑10 coding for diagnoses, as required by many schemes and regulated by national standards. These requirements reinforce the need for robust Service, FeeSchedule, Claim, and Remittance entities, as well as configuration tables for per‑scheme rules and mappings.

Ethically and legally, South African practices must bill only according to written informed consent and must ensure that guideline tariffs and actual charged fees are aligned with patient expectations. The system should thus provide clear treatment estimates and informed consent workflows, similar to ExACT SA’s treatment estimates and EXACT’s pictorial estimates with images and Clinipad signatures.[24][24][9] Storing signed estimates and consent documents as PDFs linked to patients and COTs provides evidence that patients were informed of costs and treatment plans, which can be important in dispute resolution. Audit trails for invoice and claim changes are also essential, ensuring that any resubmission of treatments or adjustments to invoices are traceable in terms of who made the change and why, echoing EXACT’s resubmission audit records and adjustment logs.[37][41]

### 5.5 Drawing Design Inspiration: Combining the Strengths of Both Exact Systems

The two Exact products, while differently branded and targeted, jointly provide a rich template for a custom South African system. ExACT SA’s key strength lies in its deep integration with South African medical aid billing: its fees importer for scheme tariffs, its explicit support for treatment plan proposals and claims to medical insurance, and its emphasis on managing nearly all financial flows in the practice.[9][49][9][9] For a custom system, this translates into prioritising robust tariff management, scheme membership modelling, claim generation and remittance posting, and providing tools to keep scheme fee schedules up to date. EXACT SOE’s principal strengths, conversely, are in clinical charting sophistication, course‑of‑treatment‑based planning, intuitive appointment and arrival workflows, automated recalls, patient portal integration, and a comprehensive reporting framework.[15][16][38][16] For a custom system, this suggests investing in a high‑quality odontogram interface, flexible multi‑plan treatment structures, recall automation tied to appointment and treatment history, and a rich suite of financial and clinical reports.

In practice, an optimal architecture for a single South African dental practice would combine ExACT SA’s revenue‑cycle prowess with EXACT’s clinical and engagement capabilities. The data model sketched earlier, incorporating patients, families, scheme memberships, courses of treatment, treatment items, fee schedules, invoices, claims and remittances, provides the backbone for such a system. Building user workflows on top of this backbone — from family appointments and arrival screens with portal forms to course‑of‑treatment planning panels and claim submission dashboards — will allow the practice to achieve the level of integration and automation typified by modern PMS products.[11][21][9][6]

## 6. Conclusion

A careful examination of ExACT Dental Software (South Africa) and EXACT by Software of Excellence reveals that, despite different branding and regional focus, both systems embody a common architectural logic for dental practice management. They treat the dental practice as an integrated organism in which patient identity, family and payor relationships, clinical findings, treatment plans, and financial transactions are tightly linked in a single data model. ExACT SA distinguishes itself in the South African context through its strong emphasis on patient billing, medical aid tariffs, and EDI claim submissions, supported by a fees importer for scheme‑specific tariffs, treatment estimates and scheme pre‑authorisation proposals, and comprehensive handling of invoices, receipts, and statements.[9][49][9][9] EXACT SOE, by contrast, excels in clinical charting and treatment planning with advanced odontogram features and course‑of‑treatment structures, robust appointment and arrival workflows, automated recalls, patient portal integration, and a rich reporting framework covering financial, clinical and marketing dimensions.[15][16][38][16][29]

For a developer building a custom system for a single South African dental practice, the key design lessons from these products are clear. The patient record must be relationally linked to families and scheme memberships, explicitly modelling main members and dependants, and distinguishing clinical subjects from financial guarantors.[21][22][22] Treatment planning should pivot around a course‑of‑treatment entity that groups treatment items, carries payor and authorisation context, and serves as the bridge between clinical charting and billing, with treatment items linked to service codes and fee schedules.[9][36][5] Billing processes must transform completed treatment items into invoice lines by applying scheme or private fee schedules, separating patient and payor portions, and generating both patient invoices and claims where appropriate, with adjustments handled via new ledger entries rather than overwriting history.[39][41][49][9] Claims handling should integrate with South African switching houses using EDI, tracking claim and remittance identifiers and matching scheme payments to invoice lines, while patient statements and reports should provide transparent views of charges, payments, and outstanding balances at both patient and scheme levels.[21][25][21][42][17][2]

By combining ExACT SA’s tariff and claims‑handling patterns with EXACT’s sophisticated clinical and engagement workflows, and by grounding the design in sound relational principles of primary and foreign keys as outlined in ERD best practices, a custom South African dental practice management system can be built that is both locally compliant and functionally competitive with established products.[33] Such a system will enable the practice not only to chart and treat patients efficiently but also to navigate the complexities of medical aid billing, reduce claim rejections, automate recalls and communications, and generate actionable analytics, thereby aligning technological infrastructure with the practice’s clinical and financial goals.
