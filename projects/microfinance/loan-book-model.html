Structure

Who carries the insurance risk

The single largest structural decision, and it is a capital decision before it is a risk one. Your own microinsurance licence is gated behind a R4m capital floor — which at a R1m–R5m budget would consume the entire loan book. A cell captive gets you the underwriting profit for R250 000.

Insurance

Credit life and the funeral book

Credit life is capped at R4.50 per R1 000 of the deferred amount and rides on loans you have already written. The funeral book is independent of the loan book entirely — and at scale it is the larger business.

Credit life on the loan book

Premium accrues on the outstanding balance each month, so an amortising loan earns less than its face value suggests. Borrowers may substitute their own policy at any time, which is where the take-up assumption bites.

Funeral book

South Africa's largest insurance category by policy count, roughly 30 million policies in force. Nearly a quarter lapse within the first year — which is precisely why selling into a book that already has a working debit order beats selling cold.

Assumptions you must verify before committing capital

The credit rate-cap formula is contested in public sources. Practitioner commentary splits between (repo × 2.2) + 20% and repo + 21% for unsecured credit — a gap of roughly seven percentage points of gross yield. This model uses the multiplier form and exposes it as an input. Confirm against the Government Gazette before pricing a single loan.

Credit life is a conduct-risk product, and the regulator is watching it. Claims ratios ran under 10% across the market — roughly R15bn of annual premium against R1.5bn of claims — which is exactly what provoked the 2017 cap. Assume the R4.50 ceiling is revisited downward, not upward, and do not build a plan that only works at today's margin. Borrowers also hold a statutory right to substitute their own policy at any time, so a high take-up assumption is a commercial assumption, not a given.

Funeral economics live or die on lapse. Roughly a quarter of funeral policies do not survive the first year, and a comparable share lapse before the first premium is ever collected. Acquisition cost is therefore incurred repeatedly on the same customer. The model charges replacement acquisition every year against the lapsed share — if your real lapse rate exceeds the input, the funeral engine degrades faster than any other line here.

Not modelled, and each one is real: the in duplum rule; reckless-lending exposure under sections 80–83, which can void an agreement entirely; FICA obligations; POPIA; mandatory credit bureau reporting; Debt Collectors Act registration for in-house collections; FAIS licensing, key-individual competency and representative supervision for selling any insurance product; and Treating Customers Fairly conduct standards, which apply with particular force to exactly these two products.

Distribution is not modelled, and it is the real constraint on the funeral book. The model charges acquisition cost per policy and admin per policy, but carries no sales force, branch network, call centre or agent commission structure. A book of several thousand policies is a distribution business before it is an underwriting one, and the cost of building that channel is absent from every figure here. Treat large policy counts as showing the shape of the opportunity, not a plan you can fund at these margins.

This is a commercial model, not legal or actuarial advice. The licensing path needs sign-off from a credit-regulatory attorney, and the insurance pricing from a qualified actuary, before a rand goes out.
